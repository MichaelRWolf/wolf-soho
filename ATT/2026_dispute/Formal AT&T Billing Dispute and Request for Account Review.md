In January 2025, we visited an AT&T store in Dunnellon, Florida, to replace our aging iPhones.

While there, we asked whether AT&T could offer us a better service arrangement than the approximately $150/month we were already paying.

The salesperson offered a package costing approximately **$130/month total**, including service for our two new phones and a third line for our existing portable Wi-Fi hotspot. (We returned within a few days to pickup our phones and have the technician set-up our Hotspot).

To qualify, we were told we needed to surrender an existing discount arrangement that also included an entertainment benefit. We agreed, relying on the promise of a lower overall monthly cost.

We also **explicitly declined AT&T Next Up**, as we have no interest in frequent phone upgrades.

The salesperson warned us that our first bill would be unusually high because of initial charges, but assured us that subsequent bills would reflect the lower recurring price.

Instead, our recurring bills were substantially higher than the amount promised. By mid-2025, they exceeded $200/month, eventually reaching approximately $265-270/month.

We also discovered that we had been charged $10/month per phone for Next Up Anytime, despite explicitly declining this service.

**We dispute the charges exceeding the original agreed-upon arrangement and the unauthorized Next Up charges.
